# How Should You Prepare for an Immigration Consultation About Moving to Canada

Moving to Canada is a major life decision. Whether you are planning to build a career, continue your education, reunite with your family, or apply for permanent residency, understanding the immigration process is an important first step.

For many people, the journey begins with an immigration consultation. Speaking with an immigration professional can help you understand your eligibility, explore available immigration programmes, identify the documents you need, and plan your next steps.

However, simply booking a consultation is not enough. Preparing before your appointment can make the discussion more useful and help you get answers to the questions that matter most.

This is particularly important for French-speaking individuals and families who want to move to Canada. You may have questions about Express Entry, French-language immigration opportunities, Provincial Nominee Programmes, work permits, or permanent residency.

You might also be unsure whether your educational qualifications, professional experience, and language skills meet Canadian immigration requirements.

So, how can you prepare for an immigration consultation and make the most of your meeting?

In this guide, Asra Immigration ([asraimmigration.ca)](https://canada.asraimmigration.ca/) explains everything you should know before your consultation, including the documents to prepare, important questions to ask, common mistakes to avoid, and practical steps to take before starting your Canadian immigration journey.

## 1. Understand Why You Want to Move to Canada

Before attending an immigration consultation, take some time to identify your main reason for moving to Canada.

Your immigration goals will help the professional understand your situation and identify potentially suitable pathways.

For example, you may be interested in:

- Applying for Canadian permanent residency.
- Finding professional employment in Canada.
- Studying at a Canadian university or college.
- Joining your spouse or family members.
- Exploring immigration opportunities for French-speaking professionals.
- Visiting Canada before making a long-term decision.
- Exploring eligible business immigration opportunities.

You do not need to know which immigration programme you qualify for before the meeting. Identifying the right pathway is one of the things you can discuss during your consultation.

However, having a clear goal makes it easier to explain what you want to achieve.

For instance, someone interested in permanent residency may need to explore Express Entry or provincial immigration pathways, while someone planning to pursue higher education may need information about study permits and designated learning institutions.

Write down your main reasons for choosing Canada and what you hope to achieve after moving.

## 2. Prepare Your Personal and Family Information

Your personal circumstances can influence your eligibility for different Canadian immigration programmes.

During the consultation, you may be asked about your age, nationality, marital status, family members, and current country of residence.

Prepare the following information:

- Full name and date of birth.
- Country of citizenship.
- Current country of residence.
- Marital status.
- Details about your spouse or partner.
- Number and ages of dependent children.
- Information about eligible family members living in Canada.
- Your preferred Canadian province or city.

If you are planning to immigrate with your family, prepare information about your spouse's education, employment history, and language abilities as well.

These details may help determine whether applying as a family or including your spouse as an accompanying applicant could be appropriate.

Be honest about your circumstances. Even small details can sometimes affect immigration eligibility or documentation requirements.

## 3. Organise Your Educational Documents

Education is an important consideration in several Canadian immigration programmes, particularly those designed for skilled workers.

Before your consultation, collect information about your academic background.

Useful documents include:

- School completion certificates.
- College or university degrees.
- Academic transcripts.
- Professional qualifications.
- Graduation certificates.
- Details about the institutions where you studied.

If you completed your education outside Canada, ask whether you need an Educational Credential Assessment (ECA).

An ECA evaluates how your foreign educational qualification compares with Canadian educational standards for immigration purposes.

For example, if you completed a bachelor's or master's degree in France, India, Belgium, Senegal, or another country, an ECA may be required for certain economic immigration pathways.

Not every immigration programme has identical education requirements, so avoid paying for an assessment before confirming whether it is needed for your chosen pathway.

## 4. Prepare Your Employment History and Professional Experience

Your professional experience may play a significant role in determining which Canadian immigration programmes you can explore.

If you are considering Express Entry or a Provincial Nominee Programme, the details of your previous employment may be particularly important.

Prepare a clear employment history that includes:

- Current and previous job titles.
- Employer names and locations.
- Employment start and end dates.
- Main job responsibilities.
- Full-time or part-time employment details.
- Average weekly working hours.
- Salary information.
- Relevant professional qualifications.

If available, bring employment reference letters, experience certificates, and supporting salary records.

One important point is that Canadian immigration authorities may assess your actual job duties rather than relying only on your job title.

For example, two applicants working as project managers may have different responsibilities. Explaining your daily duties can help determine the appropriate occupational classification.

If you have employment gaps, freelance experience, or previous self-employment, mention these details during the consultation.

A complete and accurate employment history can help the professional assess your background more effectively.

## 5. Understand Your French and English Language Skills

For French-speaking applicants, language proficiency can be an important part of Canadian immigration planning.

Canada has immigration pathways and selection measures that recognize French-language abilities, including Express Entry's French-language proficiency category.

Before your consultation, consider your current abilities in:

- Speaking.
- Listening.
- Reading.
- Writing.

If you have already taken a language test, bring your results.

Approved French-language tests for relevant immigration purposes include:

- **TEF Canada:** Test d'évaluation de français.
- **TCF Canada:** Test de connaissance du français.

Approved English tests may also be useful depending on your immigration programme. These include IELTS General Training, CELPIP-General, and PTE Core.

For the Express Entry French-language proficiency category, candidates generally need to demonstrate NCLC Level 7 or higher in all four language abilities and meet the other applicable Express Entry requirements.

Ask your immigration professional whether taking both French and English tests could strengthen your profile.

If you have not taken a language test yet, your consultation can help you understand which examination may be suitable and what scores you should aim for.

## 6. Gather Your Previous Immigration Records

If you have previously applied for a Canadian visa, study permit, work permit, or permanent residency, make sure you mention it during your consultation.

Your previous immigration history can be important when assessing your current options.

Prepare copies of:

- Previous Canadian visa applications.
- Visa approval letters.
- Refusal letters.
- Previous work or study permits.
- Visitor visa records.
- Immigration correspondence.
- Details of previous visa refusals from other countries, where relevant.
- Information about previous overstays or immigration status issues.

If you have received a Canadian visa refusal, bring the refusal letter and explain what happened.

Do not hide previous refusals or provide incomplete information. An immigration professional needs to understand your full history to assess your situation properly.

For example, if your previous visitor visa was refused because of concerns about your financial circumstances or travel purpose, this information may be relevant when considering a future application.

Being honest during the consultation helps avoid misunderstandings and allows the professional to provide more meaningful guidance.

## 7. Prepare Your Financial Information

Financial preparation is another important part of planning your move to Canada.

Depending on your immigration pathway, you may need to demonstrate that you have sufficient funds to support yourself and your accompanying family members.

Before the consultation, prepare a general overview of your financial situation.

You may want to collect:

- Recent bank statements.
- Savings information.
- Employment income details.
- Salary slips.
- Investment information, where relevant.
- Information about available family support.
- Details of any financial sponsorship, if applicable.

You do not necessarily need every financial document for an initial consultation. However, having a realistic understanding of your available funds can help the professional explain which immigration options may be suitable.

For example, applicants considering the Federal Skilled Worker Program may need to meet settlement fund requirements unless they qualify for an exemption.

International students may also need to demonstrate that they can cover tuition fees, living expenses, and other required costs.

Ask which financial documents are relevant to your particular application rather than assuming that every immigration programme has identical requirements.

## 8. Research Canadian Immigration Programmes You May Qualify For

You do not need to become an immigration expert before your consultation. However, having a basic understanding of available programmes can help you participate more confidently.

Here are some immigration pathways worth discussing.

### Express Entry

Express Entry manages applications for several economic immigration programmes, including:

- Federal Skilled Worker Program.
- Canadian Experience Class.
- Federal Skilled Trades Program.

It uses the Comprehensive Ranking System (CRS) to rank eligible candidates.

If you are a skilled professional with foreign work experience, ask whether the Federal Skilled Worker Program could be suitable for your profile.

### Provincial Nominee Programmes

Canadian provinces and territories have immigration pathways designed to attract people with skills and experience that match their economic needs.

Some programmes require a job offer, while others may have different eligibility criteria.

Ask whether your education, occupation, and professional experience match any available provincial opportunities.

### French-Language Immigration Opportunities

French-speaking applicants may have additional opportunities through Canada's immigration initiatives supporting Francophone communities.

For example, eligible Express Entry candidates may benefit from French-language category-based selection.

If you are interested in living outside Quebec, ask about immigration pathways that support Francophone newcomers in other Canadian provinces.

### Quebec Immigration

If your goal is to settle in Quebec, research the province's separate immigration selection system.

Quebec has its own immigration programmes and requirements, so the process may differ from federal economic immigration pathways.

Understanding these basic differences will help you ask more focused questions during your consultation.

## 9. Write Down Important Questions Before Your Consultation

One of the best ways to prepare for an immigration consultation is to create a list of questions in advance.

It is easy to forget important details when discussing something as significant as moving to another country.

Here are some useful questions to consider.

**Questions about eligibility:**

- Which Canadian immigration programmes may suit my background?
- Do I qualify for Express Entry?
- What are the main strengths and weaknesses of my profile?
- Can my spouse's qualifications improve our immigration profile?
- Are there immigration opportunities specifically for French speakers?

**Questions about documents:**

- Do I need an Educational Credential Assessment?
- Which language test should I take?
- What employment documents should I prepare?
- Do my French-language documents need translation?
- Will I need proof of settlement funds?

**Questions about costs and timelines:**

- What are the expected government application fees?
- What are your professional service fees?
- Are there additional expenses?
- What are the current estimated processing times?
- What could cause delays in my application?

**Questions about professional services:**

- Are you authorized to provide paid immigration advice?
- Who will handle my application?
- What services are included in your package?
- Will I receive copies of submitted documents?
- How will you communicate with me throughout the process?

You do not have to ask every question. Prioritise the ones that are most important to your circumstances.

## 10. Be Honest About Your Immigration History and Personal Circumstances

An immigration consultation is most useful when you provide accurate and complete information.

Some applicants feel uncomfortable discussing previous refusals, employment gaps, financial difficulties, or complicated family situations.

However, leaving out important information can lead to incorrect advice.

Be prepared to discuss:

- Previous immigration refusals.
- Gaps in employment.
- Changes in marital status.
- Previous travel history.
- Criminal or legal issues that may affect admissibility.
- Previous immigration violations.
- Changes in your financial circumstances.
- Any concerns about the authenticity or availability of documents.

You do not need to feel embarrassed about your circumstances.

A professional consultation should be an opportunity to understand your options, including any challenges that may need to be addressed.

Never agree to hide information or provide false documents to improve your chances of approval.

## 11. Understand the Consultant's Fees and Services Before Agreeing

Before hiring an immigration professional, make sure you understand what the consultation includes.

Some consultations focus on general eligibility and immigration options, while others may include a detailed review of your documents or a written assessment.

Ask about:

- The consultation fee.
- The duration of the meeting.
- Whether the consultation is online or in person.
- Which documents will be reviewed.
- Whether you will receive a written summary.
- Whether application preparation is included.
- What additional services may cost.

If you are hiring a paid immigration consultant, verify that they are authorized to provide Canadian immigration advice.

A regulated Canadian immigration consultant should be a member in good standing of the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC). Canadian lawyers and Quebec notaries may also provide immigration services within their authorized professional scope.

If you decide to proceed with further services, request a written agreement explaining the work to be provided and the associated fees.

Do not sign blank forms or agreements you do not understand.

## 12. Make Notes and Understand Your Next Steps

Once the consultation is finished, take some time to review everything discussed.

You should have a clearer understanding of your potential immigration options and what needs to happen next.

Write down important points such as:

- Which immigration programmes were discussed.
- Which programme may be most suitable.
- What additional documents you need.
- Whether you need language testing.
- Whether you need an ECA.
- Any eligibility concerns identified.
- Expected expenses.
- Important deadlines.
- Your next preparation steps.

If something is unclear, ask for clarification rather than making assumptions.

You should also understand that an initial consultation does not automatically mean you have appointed the consultant to represent you throughout the immigration process. Further services should be clearly agreed upon in writing.

A good consultation should leave you with a realistic understanding of your situation, not simply a promise that your application will be approved.

## 13. How to Make Your Immigration Consultation More Productive

Apart from preparing your documents and questions, a few simple steps can help you get more value from your appointment.

**Be organised:** Keep your important documents in clearly labelled digital folders so you can find them easily during an online meeting.

**Be specific:** Instead of simply saying you want to move to Canada, explain whether you are interested in permanent residency, employment, education, or family sponsorship.

**Be realistic:** Understand that eligibility and final approval depend on immigration rules and government decisions.

**Ask for explanations:** If you do not understand an immigration term or requirement, ask the consultant to explain it in simple language.

**Take notes:** Record important recommendations, documents required, and suggested next steps.

**Involve your spouse if necessary:** If you are planning to immigrate together, having your spouse participate can help ensure that both of you understand the available options.

For French-speaking applicants, discussing the consultation in a language you understand comfortably can also help avoid communication problems.

## 14. Why French-Speaking Applicants Should Prepare Carefully

Canada offers several immigration opportunities for French-speaking individuals, but choosing the right pathway requires an understanding of the applicable requirements.

For example, French proficiency can be valuable under certain Express Entry selection measures.

Applicants may also want to explore opportunities to settle in Francophone communities outside Quebec.

During your consultation, consider discussing:

- Your current French proficiency.
- Whether you should take TEF Canada or TCF Canada.
- Whether taking an English test could improve your profile.
- French-language category-based selection.
- Provincial immigration opportunities.
- Francophone Mobility for eligible temporary employment outside Quebec.
- Quebec's separate immigration selection process.

You should also discuss your preferred destination.

Living in Quebec may involve different immigration procedures from settling in Ontario, New Brunswick, Manitoba, or another province.

A consultation that considers your language abilities and preferred destination can help you understand which opportunities deserve further investigation.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

**1. What should I bring to a Canadian immigration consultation?**

Bring your passport, educational certificates, employment records, language test results, previous immigration documents, and relevant financial information. The exact documents depend on your immigration goals.

**2. Do I need to know which Canadian immigration programme I qualify for before the consultation?**

No. Identifying suitable immigration pathways is one of the main purposes of an immigration consultation. However, having a basic understanding of your goals and background will make the discussion more productive.

**3. Should I bring my spouse to the immigration consultation?**

If you are planning to immigrate together, involving your spouse can be helpful. Their education, employment history, language abilities, and personal circumstances may also influence your immigration options.

**4. Should French-speaking applicants take TEF Canada or TCF Canada?**

Both are approved French-language tests for relevant Canadian immigration programmes. Your choice may depend on your preferences, availability, and preparation. Discuss the applicable language requirements before booking a test.

**5. How long does an immigration consultation usually take?**

The duration depends on the consultant and the services offered. Some initial consultations last around 30 minutes, while more detailed assessments may require longer appointments.

**6. Can I attend a Canadian immigration consultation online?**

Yes. Many immigration professionals offer online consultations through video calls or other communication platforms. This allows applicants living outside Canada to discuss their options without travelling.

**7. Should I hire an immigration consultant immediately after the first meeting?**

No. Take time to review the advice, verify the professional's authorization, understand the fees, and read the written service agreement before deciding whether to proceed.

**8. Can an immigration consultant guarantee my Canadian PR approval?**

No. Immigration consultants cannot guarantee approval because final decisions are made by the relevant Canadian immigration authorities. Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed permanent residency.
